## Backstory

Delia grew up behind the counter of her mother's diner in Joliet, where she learned that a story only matters if the regulars will read it. Medill and the public affairs reporting program in Springfield put her in the Illinois statehouse for the Chicago Tribune at twenty-three. In the pressroom she traded gossip and the occasional scoop with a sharp young Senate press secretary named Rosalind Ferraro.

She moved to Washington to cover power markets for Platts, where an editor taught her to call back every source before filing. That editor still calls her to check a quote. In 2011 she profiled Gilbert Faraday's procurement program at Digital Realty, and he made her rewrite the headline twice. Four years at Utility Dive followed, covering the grid as data centers began to show up in rate cases.

She crossed over to run media relations at Exelon through outages, rate cases and the first wave of large-load requests. That is where Constance Wright challenged a press release figure she had approved. She has written the headline and she has been quoted in it. She is on the panel because she can tell you how a sentence will look on page one before you say it.
