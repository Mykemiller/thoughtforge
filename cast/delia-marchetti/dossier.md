## Backstory

Delia grew up behind the counter of her mother's diner in Joliet, where she learned that a story only matters if the regulars will read it. Medill and the public affairs reporting program in Springfield put her in the Illinois statehouse at twenty-three. There she shared a pressroom, cab fare and the occasional scoop with an AP reporter named Rosalind Ferraro.

She covered power markets for The Wall Street Journal in Houston after Enron. Then she led the energy and utilities team at Bloomberg, where Constance Wright was the standards editor who killed her favorite lede. In 2011 she profiled Gilbert Faraday's procurement program at Digital Realty, and he made her rewrite the headline twice.

She crossed over to run media relations at Exelon, then built an energy and infrastructure practice at Edelman. She has written the headline and she has been quoted in it. She is on the panel because she can tell you how a sentence will look on page one before you say it.
