## Backstory

Albert grew up in Mansfield, Ohio, where his father worked at the steel mill and a rate increase was a dinner-table topic. He studied economics at Ohio State and wrote a Wisconsin dissertation on how regulators price what they cannot see.

He spent six years as a staff economist at the Public Utilities Commission of Ohio, then nine at PJM building the interconnection queue process he now critiques. A stint advising at FERC taught him the difference between what a rule says and how it gets applied. At E3 he led the 2016 grid-impact study Gilbert Faraday sponsored for Microsoft. Its conclusion was the one Gilbert did not want, and it held. In a 2018 FERC proceeding he testified for a state-commission coalition while Hartford Caldwell sat across the room for the developer.

At Resources for the Future he measured how long queues really take. He is unflappable because he has seen every version of the argument before, usually from the other seat.
