## Backstory

Hart grew up in Richmond. His mother taught civics and made him read the county code aloud at dinner, which he now calls the best legal training he ever had. He studied history at the University of Virginia, law at Georgetown and tax at NYU, then spent eighteen years at Hunton & Williams working on utility regulation, siting and incentive agreements.

Six years in Dominion's legal department showed him the utility's side of every argument he would later make against it. At Latham & Watkins he represented developers through permitting and incentive negotiations. That included a 2018 FERC proceeding where Albert Thorne testified for the other side, a 2019 abatement deal he modeled overnight with Victoria Stroud, and a 2020 central Ohio rezoning where Rosalind Ferraro talked him into conceding on water. She was right.

At QTS he worked the incentives and power agreements behind campus development. On the panel he opens with the exposure and closes with the mitigation, and he will stop a recommendation that creates legal or consent risk.
