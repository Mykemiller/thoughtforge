## Backstory

Mach grew up over his family's machine shop outside Stuttgart, where tolerances were measured in microns and opinions were not rewarded. He took his first engineering degree at the Technical University of Munich, then a master's in power systems at Georgia Tech, where he learned American utility practice and American directness.

At Siemens he designed HVDC converters and met a young American strategist named Gilbert Faraday. The two argued about an interconnector bid for four years and have argued ever since. He moved to ABB in Raleigh to work on medium-voltage DC, then spent fifteen years at Burns & McDonnell as the electrical engineer who told hyperscale clients what physics would and would not allow. He once told a client's chief executive, in a design review, that the plan was physically wrong. The client changed the plan and hired him again.

In 2019 he was owner's engineer on a Reno-area campus that Margo Brandt was building. From 2021 to 2023 he was engineer of record on a liquid-cooled Midwest campus that Victoria Stroud financed and Ingrid Halverson reviewed. At Meta he worked on 800 VDC rack power and sat in OCP working groups. He is usually early and usually right, and he knows which of those annoys people more.
