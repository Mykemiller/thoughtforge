## Backstory

Gilbert grew up on the Tacoma waterfront, where his father kept cranes and lights running through Puget Sound winters. He learned early that the physical world does not care about the slide deck. An engineering degree at the University of Washington led to transmission planning at Puget Sound Power & Light, then four years at McKinsey, where he learned to say the important thing first.

A mid-career MPA took him to Siemens in Munich, where European grid politics taught him that every technical answer is also a regulatory one, and where he met a young power-electronics engineer named Mach Eigen who was certain about everything and usually right. Tokyo followed, and with it a patience for long infrastructure cycles.

He came home to the data center industry just as it began to need power more than space: power procurement at Digital Realty, then seven years running energy and site development at Microsoft, where Margo Brandt worked for him and argued with him on schedule. At Brattle he advised the people on the other side of the table. He chairs the ThoughtForge panel the way he ran those rooms. He lets everyone argue, waits for Constance to grade the claims, and then rules in one sentence.
